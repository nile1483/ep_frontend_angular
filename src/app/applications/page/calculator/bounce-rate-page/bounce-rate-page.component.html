Bounce Rate

Calculator

Using Excellent Publicity’s Bounce Rate Calculator or Site Engagement Meter can help you understand visitor engagement with your website page. It shows the overall performance of your website, presenting the bounce rate of your website by users.

  • What is bounce rate?

Bounce rate presents the percentage of visitors who arrive on a web page and quit without further engagement. If your website’s bounce rate goes high, that means there is content irrelevance, slow loading times, or an unsatisfactory user experience.

This indicator is frequently employed to gauge a website’s level of engagement. According to the result gained by this metric, if the bounce rate is higher, that means your website’s engagement ratio is lower. Using a Bounce rate calculator can help you analyze and rectify the leading causes.

As e-mail marketing is also one of the ways to increase the engagement of your website pages, using the E-mail Bounce Rate Calculator can assist you in measuring the effectiveness of your website through analysis, monitoring and optimization.

  • How to calculate the bounce rate?

If you want to calculate the bounce rate of your website, there is a bounce rate formula that you can utilize. You can calculate the number of bounces or single-page visits to the overall number of visitors, which will give you insights into your website’s gross performance during a certain tenure.

Bounce Rate Calculator:

Bounce Rate = (Total Bounces / Total Visitors) × 100

Let’s understand:

  • Total Bounces: It shows the number of people who view a website and quit it before making any further activities.
  • Total Visitors: It represents the number of visitors who engage with the website for a particular period.

For example, while a total of 1000 visitors come to engage with the website and 600 of them head out without making any interaction, the bounce rate will be:

= 600 / 1000 × 100

= 60%

The high bounce rate is an indication that your website performance is sluggish, presence of irrelevant content, or poor user experience.

  • Types of Bounces

In terms of website analytics, three types of bounce rates show the engagement performance of visitors:

  • Hard Bounce: Hard bounce takes place when visitors enter the webpage but quit it in no time without engaging further. This shows that users are minimally interactive with slight scrolling or avoid scrolling, indicating little or zero consumer interest.
  • Medium Bounce: Medium Bounce visitors eventually spend a short time on the page before scrolling away. Though they are not your target audience, they can probably become one-time shoppers who will come back and purchase sometime down the line.
  • Soft Bounce: Soft bounce visitors spend a required amount of time on the landing page overall, exploring the content simply by scrolling, clicking, and surfing through various sections. Even while interacting with websites, they end up not doing what they are meant to do. They show their interest, but something prevents them from converting or engaging with more of the website content.
  • What causes high email bounce rates?

There are many reasons for occurring high bounce rates of your website that indicate different user behaviour. The following are some key types:

  • Invalid or Fake Email Addresses: Typing errors, old addresses, or phoney emails can lead to hard bounces.
  • Spam Filters: Emails that cover an abundance of links, terrible formatting, and inappropriate content may be flagged as spam and restricted.
  • Crowded Mailboxes: You can receive new emails if the inbox of recipient is full.
  • Server errors: Delivery Interruption can occur from the recipient’s or server’s mail servers.
  • Blocked IPs or Domains: If mail is sent through negatively reputed IPs or domains, it can get banned.

Minimizing bounce rates is crucial in terms of elevating the quality of email performance. It’s advisable to clean your inboxes, verification before sending, double opt-in verification should be done, and content must comply with best practices to stay away from spam filters in terms of bounce rate reduction.
